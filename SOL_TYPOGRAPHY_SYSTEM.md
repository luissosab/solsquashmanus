# Sol Squash Typography System

This document governs typography across every public route. **Copy role—not page, color block, or personal preference—determines family, case, scale, weight, and emphasis.** Background colors may change text color for contrast, but they do not change the hierarchy.

| Copy role | Family and treatment | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Eyebrow or section label | Archivo Narrow, small, bold, tracked uppercase | Identifies the section. Cyan, mustard, green, navy, or white may be used only for contrast. Never italic. |
| Page or section heading | Archivo Narrow, largest scale, bold uppercase | Establishes the primary hierarchy. May use a contrasting Sol color on selected words, but never changes family or becomes italic. |
| Emotional supporting line | DM Serif Display, sentence case, italic | Reserved for one deliberate emotional line in selected narrative contexts. It must remain smaller than the section heading. |
| Decision-card title | Archivo Narrow, large, bold uppercase | Used for major choices and expandable play options. One consistent scale tier. Never italic. |
| Content-card title | Archivo Narrow, medium, bold uppercase | Used for programme, profile, value, session, FAQ, and pricing-card titles. One consistent scale tier. Never italic. |
| Card supporting line | Manrope, bold sentence case | May use mustard or another contrast-safe Sol accent. Never italic. |
| Body copy | Manrope, regular sentence case | Uses one readable size and line-height range. Never italic. |
| CTA or action label | Archivo Narrow, small, bold, tracked uppercase | Applies to buttons, text links, and disclosure actions. Never italic. |

## Controlled Exceptions

The approved emotional supporting-line treatment currently appears in three related narrative contexts: the Home Club promise, the Our Story journey narrative, and the sitewide Club footer promise. Existing colored words inside major headings remain **display-sans uppercase**, not emotional italics. This keeps color expressive while preserving a recognizable brand grammar.
